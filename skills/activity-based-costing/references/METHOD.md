# Activity-Based Costing: method

All business illustrations and figures are synthetic.

**Core formulas**

- `Activity rate = activity cost pool ÷ total activity-driver volume`.
- `Object activity cost = object driver units × activity rate`.
- `Object full cost = direct costs + sum of assigned activity costs`.

**Decision process**

1. Define objects, resource pools and activities.
2. Match the driver to the consumption logic.
3. Reconcile allocated totals with source pools.
4. Compare to a volume-only method and interpret differences, without assuming ABC is necessarily causal or superior.

**Worked synthetic example**

Two pools: order handling €84,000 across 420 orders (€200/order), and equipment support €198,000 across 9,000 machine hours (€22/hour). Product A causes 140 orders and 2,800 machine hours; assigned cost is `140×€200 + 2,800×€22 = €89,600`.

**Common traps**: ignoring non-unit drivers, counting the same overhead twice, or reporting total cost as a per-unit rate.
