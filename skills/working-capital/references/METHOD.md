# Working Capital & Liquidity: method

All business illustrations and figures are synthetic.

**Core formulas**

- `Net working capital = current assets − current liabilities`.
- `Days sales outstanding = average trade receivables ÷ credit sales × days in period`.
- `Days inventory outstanding = average inventory ÷ cost of sales × days`.
- `Days payables outstanding = average trade payables ÷ cost of sales × days`.
- `Cash conversion cycle = DSO + DIO − DPO`.

**Decision process**

1. Define accounting scope and average-versus-ending balances.
2. Use matching annualized or period-specific denominators.
3. Diagnose ageing, stock availability and supplier concentration.
4. Model cash effects and operational trade-offs before recommending shorter cycles.

**Worked synthetic example**

DSO 44 days, DIO 57 days, DPO 38 days. Cash conversion cycle = `44 + 57 − 38 = 63 days`. Decreasing inventory days is attractive only if fulfilment and continuity remain acceptable.

**Common traps**: treating a positive working capital balance as proof of cash availability; comparing ratios with unmatched time bases.
