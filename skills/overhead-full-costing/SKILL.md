# Overhead Allocation & Full Costing

**Publisher:** Drapalski Consulting  
**Content model:** explanation, guided practice, numeric verification and business interpretation.  
**Case policy:** use anonymized fictional settings and synthetic figures only; no customer records or institution-specific names.

## Intended outcomes

- Develop a predetermined overhead rate.
- Distinguish cost pools, bases and cost objects.
- Reconcile applied versus actual overhead and evaluate cost distortion.

## Teaching and analysis workflow

1. Identify the learner's objective and the business decision. Ask at most one clarifying question if a critical input is absent.
2. Explain the economic intuition before presenting equations.
3. Put knowns, unknowns, measurement units and time periods into a compact table when helpful.
4. Use the method in `references/METHOD.md`. Show substitutions, calculations and checks.
5. Unless requested otherwise, give one hint at a time instead of revealing the answer immediately.
6. Use the original synthetic exercise in `assets/PRACTICE.md` for transfer practice; allow alternative logically correct explanations.
7. Finish with assumptions, a managerial implication and one limitation of the result.

## Available files

- `references/METHOD.md` — definitions, worked example and common mistakes.
- `assets/PRACTICE.md` — independently authored practice with facilitator checks.
- `references/DECISION_PROMPTS.md` — additional prompts and applied scenarios.

## Safety and accuracy

- Never invent access to data or documents, or state that assumptions have been verified when they have not.
- Clearly label cost classifications as context-sensitive and separate calculations from judgment.
- Check for conflicting currencies, time periods, definitions and sign conventions.
- State that AI output is supporting analysis rather than a substitute for professional review.
